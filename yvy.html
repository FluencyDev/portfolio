Case study · Yvy Reciclagem

Urban mining,
made visible.

For six years I have helped one of Brazil's most specialized recycling companies show what it does: online, at trade shows, in print, and in the gift its partners receive at the end of each year.

Photo: Yvy Reciclagem. All rights reserved.

The website

One company, two conversations

The brand was already in place when Yvy hired me to build its website. My part was to make a technical business easy to understand.

Yvy talks every day with two very different people: those who generate waste and those who need recycled raw material. The site gives each one a door of their own, with a color taken from the logo, and keeps the LME quotation one click away.

I drew two diagrams to explain how it works: who Yvy serves, and what it offers.

Trade shows and visits · In person

The brand, as a place

At trade shows, the brand becomes a place. I designed the stand's walls around four values in the colors of the logo, proximity, readiness, flexibility and responsibility, with round windows onto the materials Yvy recovers. In front, a glass case puts real samples within reach, from copper wire to porcelain insulators.

For people who visit the plant, I designed a folded guide: a site map with the exit route, emergency contacts, traffic rules and the protective equipment to wear.

Year-end gift 2025 · AI first

From linear to circular

Yvy recovers value from what cities throw away. Its year-end gift had to say the same thing, and not become one more thing to throw away.

The answer was a 2026 calendar that teaches one principle of the circular economy each month, from design for disassembly to urban mining, the term Yvy uses for its own work. Every month is a word under construction: three letters built, welded, painted or taken apart by a miniature crew, the way Yvy's people handle material every day.

The object follows its own advice. The back of every sheet is lined for notes, and when the year ends, the stand becomes a clipboard or a picture frame.

How it was made

AI first, direction throughout

Each image was generated with image AI tools, five to ten iterations per month until it belonged to the same world: one scale, one light, one crew. Every image was then corrected in Photoshop, and the calendar was laid out in InDesign, with holidays, moon phases and the previous and next months on every page.

By hand, I could not have reached this look. With AI, the work moved to direction: choosing the principle, the material and the scene for each month, and discarding every result that did not belong.

Year-end gift 2026 · Hand first

Nossa Terra

Yvy is the Tupi-Guarani word for land, and the company reads its name as Nossa Terra, our land. The brief was to explore that name, so this gift starts with the land itself: Brazilian fauna and flora, from the araucaria to the anteater, drawn by hand on the iPad.

Look at the center. Between the toucan, the hummingbird and the leaves, the empty space spells YVY. The logo is not drawn: it is what the drawing leaves untouched.

The art is printed in gold on deep green, to treat Brazilian nature as something precious. The first page says it plainly: illustration made entirely by hand, without artificial intelligence.

How it was made

The opposite path

The illustration was drawn by hand on the iPad and the notebook laid out in InDesign, with the 2027 and 2028 calendars, lined pages with small drawings in the corners, and a back cover framed by the words Nossa terra. AI appears only at the end, to simulate the gold finish in a mockup for approval.

Last year, AI opened a look I could not reach by hand. This year, the idea asked for the hand, and the gift says so in print. Knowing when not to use AI is part of using it well.
